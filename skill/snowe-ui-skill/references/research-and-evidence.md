# Research and Evidence

Use when current external evidence could materially change a design decision. Research expands and tests the solution space; it is not a compulsory moodboard and never replaces product truth or rendered judgment.

## Decide Whether to Research

Research when the fact may have changed (products, standards, platform guidance, packages, browser support, font files, licenses, performance techniques, conventions); when external evidence could add or eliminate a real candidate for a high-leverage choice; when the domain carries consequential expectations of safety, regulation, accessibility, commerce or trust; when the project must understand its category before aligning with or departing from it; when an official asset, icon, typeface, component, API or source must be verified; or before inventing a costly custom solution. Skip or stop when verified repository evidence, accepted project learning or a cheap reversible prototype already answers the question, when sources only repeat familiar aesthetics, or when new evidence can no longer change the decision.

## Start With a Decision Question

"What are modern websites doing?" is not a research question. "How do current high-consideration bicycle retailers help a first-time buyer choose fit and use case before comparing price, and which approaches stay understandable on a narrow screen?" is. Before browsing, state the decision, its uncertainty, the evidence that could change it, source types, a time budget and a stop condition.

## Build an Evidence Portfolio

Mix the strongest relevant kinds rather than a long homogeneous list:

1. **Primary facts:** the repository, user-supplied analytics and research, product data, official brand material, standards, regulations, platform and package documentation.
2. **Behavioral and domain evidence:** credible research, usability findings, category mechanics, real workflows, and support material, reviews or forums when they reveal context rather than universal truth.
3. **Current product observation:** live products inspected across relevant states, widths, flows, content and failures.
4. **Adjacent models:** other domains that solve the same information, trust, comparison, spatial, editorial or interaction problem differently.
5. **Aesthetic and technical frontier:** current type, image practice, graphics, motion and browser techniques relevant to the accepted direction.

Competitors reveal category assumptions, not the answer; include at least one adjacent or structurally different model when category convergence may hide alternatives.

## Inspect Real Behavior

Never infer a product from its homepage screenshot. Inspect navigation, search, category, detail, comparison, forms, conversion, account and return paths, loading, error and empty states, pressure widths, keyboard and focus, reduced motion and performance symptoms as relevant, and record each observation as:

```text
source + date accessed
observed fact (what is actually present or happens)
context/state/viewport
inference (clearly labeled)
transferable principle or tension
decision it challenges or supports
what must not be copied
confidence and unresolved question
```

Screenshots preserve visual evidence but not effectiveness, and claims, awards, popularity or novelty do not establish usability or business value.

## Use Sources Precisely

Prefer primary and official sources for standards, platforms, APIs, packages, fonts, assets, licenses and technical behavior, and direct research reports with transparent methods over summaries for material choices. Verify dates and live behavior for unstable facts; separate standards, measured findings, conventions, expert guidance, anecdotes and your own inference; cite the exact source, never a search page. Respect copyright and licenses: extract principles, never another brand's composition, copy, signature interaction, illustration or asset system.

Useful primary starting points, used only where relevant to the platform and problem:

- W3C WCAG 2.2 understanding documents for accessibility behavior: <https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/>
- web.dev for current web performance and platform techniques: <https://web.dev/>
- Apple Human Interface Guidelines: <https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/>
- Material Design guidance: <https://m3.material.io/>
- GOV.UK Service Manual for whole-service and public-service evidence: <https://www.gov.uk/service-manual>

## Turn Research Into Candidates

Synthesize drivers and tensions, not a collage of borrowed moves:

```text
observed tension: novices choose by intended use, experts compare components
candidate A: use-case-led guided path with progressive technical depth
candidate B: browseable product field with persistent fit/use filters
candidate C: expert catalog plus a separate advisory service
```

Each candidate still needs a causal chain, a real-content prototype and a project-specific thesis; a convention can be kept for familiarity, transformed, or rejected with evidence. For type, icons, images and techniques: verify actual font files, axes, script coverage, licensing, metrics, performance and distribution before selection; inspect exact glyphs at intended size and weight; check asset ownership and whether official product media exists before generating substitutes; test frontend techniques in the target browser and stack with fallbacks, since a showcase demo is not support evidence; and when a trend matters to market perception, name its underlying composition, production method or social signal and weigh it against longer-lived drivers.

## Stop and Report

Stop when the decision can be tested with representative candidates, new sources repeat known approaches, or browsing can no longer change the outcome; a research ritual never delays a real prototype. Report only evidence that changed, constrained or meaningfully challenged the design, plus important uncertainty, and keep a short record of rejected mimicry so research does not silently become copying.

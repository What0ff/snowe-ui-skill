# Research and Evidence

Use this reference when current external evidence could materially change a design decision. Research expands and tests the solution space; it is not a compulsory moodboard and never replaces product truth or rendered judgment.

## Decide Whether to Research

Research when at least one condition is true:

- the fact may have changed: products, standards, platform guidance, packages, browser support, font files, licenses, performance techniques, or interaction conventions;
- a high-leverage choice is uncertain and external evidence could introduce or eliminate a real candidate;
- the domain has consequential user expectations, safety, regulation, accessibility, commerce, or trust requirements;
- the project needs to understand the current category before choosing how to align with or depart from it;
- an official asset, icon, typeface, component, API, or implementation source must be verified;
- the team is about to invent a costly custom solution without checking stronger existing work.

Skip or stop research when the question is already answered by verified repository evidence, accepted project learning, or a cheap reversible prototype; when sources only repeat familiar aesthetics; or when new evidence can no longer change the decision.

## Start With a Decision Question

Bad research question:

```text
What are modern websites doing?
```

Useful research question:

```text
How do current high-consideration bicycle retailers help a first-time buyer choose fit and use case before comparing price, and which approaches remain understandable on a narrow screen?
```

State the decision, current uncertainty, evidence that could change it, source types, time budget, and stop condition before browsing.

## Build an Evidence Portfolio

Use the strongest relevant mix rather than a long homogeneous list:

1. **Primary facts:** repository, analytics supplied by the user, product data, research supplied by the team, official brand material, standards, regulations, platform and package documentation.
2. **Behavioral/domain evidence:** credible research, usability findings, market/category mechanics, real workflows, support material, reviews or forums when they reveal context rather than universal truth.
3. **Current product observation:** live products and sites inspected across relevant states, widths, flows, content, and failure cases.
4. **Adjacent models:** products from other domains that solve the same information, trust, comparison, spatial, editorial, or interaction problem differently.
5. **Aesthetic/technical frontier:** current typography, image practices, graphics, motion, browser techniques, and implementation experiments relevant to the accepted direction.

Competitors reveal category assumptions, not the design answer. Include at least one adjacent or structurally different model when category convergence may be hiding alternatives.

## Inspect Real Behavior

Do not infer a product from its homepage screenshot. When relevant, inspect navigation, search, category, detail, comparison, forms, conversion, account/return paths, loading/error/empty states, mobile or pressure widths, keyboard/focus, reduced motion, and performance symptoms.

Record each observation as:

```text
source + date accessed
observed fact (what is actually present or happens)
context/state/viewport
inference (clearly labeled)
transferable principle or tension
decision it challenges or supports
what must not be copied
confidence and unresolved question
```

Screenshots can preserve visual evidence but do not prove effectiveness. Published claims, awards, popularity, or visual novelty do not automatically establish usability or business value.

## Use Sources Precisely

- Prefer primary and official sources for standards, platforms, APIs, packages, fonts, assets, licenses, and technical behavior.
- Prefer direct research reports and transparent methods over summaries when evidence affects a material choice.
- Verify dates and live behavior for unstable facts.
- Separate standards requirements, measured findings, product conventions, expert guidance, user anecdotes, and your own inference.
- Link or cite the exact source that supports a claim; do not cite a search page.
- Respect copyright and licenses. Extract principles; do not reproduce another brand's composition, copy, signature interaction, illustration, or asset system.

Useful primary starting points include:

- W3C WCAG 2.2 understanding documents for accessibility behavior: <https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/>
- web.dev for current web performance and platform techniques: <https://web.dev/>
- Apple Human Interface Guidelines: <https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/>
- Material Design guidance: <https://m3.material.io/>
- GOV.UK Service Manual for whole-service and public-service evidence: <https://www.gov.uk/service-manual>

These are evidence sources, not universal visual authorities. Use only the parts relevant to the actual platform and problem.

## Turn Research Into Candidates

Synthesize research into drivers and tensions, not a collage of borrowed moves. For example:

```text
observed tension: novices choose by intended use, experts compare components
candidate A: use-case-led guided path with progressive technical depth
candidate B: browseable product field with persistent fit/use filters
candidate C: expert catalog plus a separate advisory service
```

Each candidate still needs a causal chain, real-content prototype, and project-specific visual/interaction thesis. A current convention may be preserved for familiarity, transformed for the product, or deliberately rejected with evidence.

## Research Images, Type, Icons, and Techniques

- Verify actual font files, axes, script coverage, licensing, metrics, performance, and current distribution before selection.
- Inspect exact icon glyphs at the intended size and weight; package reputation or a catalog thumbnail is insufficient.
- Verify image/asset ownership and whether official product media exists before generating substitutes.
- Test frontend techniques in the target browser/stack and confirm fallbacks; a showcase demo is not production support evidence.
- When trends are relevant to market perception, identify the underlying composition, production method, or social signal and compare it with longer-lived product drivers.

## Stop and Report

Stop when the decision can be tested with representative candidates, new sources repeat known approaches, or further browsing cannot change the outcome. Do not delay a real prototype to complete a research ritual.

Report only evidence that changed, constrained, or meaningfully challenged the design, plus important uncertainty. Keep a short record of rejected mimicry so research does not silently become copying during implementation.

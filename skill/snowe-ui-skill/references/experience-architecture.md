# Experience Architecture

Use before art direction for new sites, products, page families, navigation systems, conversion journeys and structural redesigns, so architecture emerges from people, outcomes, content and product relationships instead of a landing-page or dashboard recipe. One page with a known job and surrounding flow is a Focused [page-hierarchy.md](page-hierarchy.md) route, not a whole-journey exercise.

## 1. Model the Whole Journey

Start outside the interface. Record the actors and their knowledge, access, motivation and responsibilities; the outcome each seeks and when the need appears; current behavior, workarounds, channels, handoffs, delays, anxieties and failures; what happens before entry and after the apparent completion; recurring use, re-entry, saved state, history and support; offline, physical, service or organizational dependencies; and the business outcome, service obligation and evidence of success. Scope the experience from this evidence, not from an org chart, database, existing transaction or competitor navigation, and make a website's boundary and handoffs intentional when it is one part of a larger journey.

## 2. Build the Object and Content Model

Before pages, identify the things users understand or act on (products, services, plans, articles, events, people, places, tasks, records, alerts, documents, orders, statuses or other domain objects); the attributes that support recognition, filtering, comparison, trust and action; relationships, quantity, variety, hierarchy, chronology, ownership, lifecycle and update frequency; required editorial, evidence, help, policy and recovery content; and missing, partial, expired, unavailable or user-generated forms. The model decides whether users need search, browsing, guided selection, comparison, sequencing, direct access, a map, a timeline or a workspace; choose those mechanisms after it.

## 3. Identify Decision Moments

For each important step: `what the user knows → what they need to know → what decision becomes possible → what could block confidence or recovery`. Evidence needs such as price, availability, fit, compatibility, status, deadline, location, demonstration, policy, review or expert support are prompts, not prescribed modules. Place conversion where intent becomes ready: neither an identical CTA on every page nor an action hidden after the user has enough evidence.

## 4. Synthesize Site or Product Topology

Generate enough architectures to cover the live organizing trade-offs. Each defines the product boundary and cross-channel handoffs; top-level areas and why they belong together; entry points for high-intent, exploratory, returning and assisted users; navigation, search, filtering, comparison, re-entry and recovery; key page jobs and transitions; the conversion or completion path; persistent context and saved work; responsive and input-mode transformations; and its strongest assumption and failure mode.

Change the organizing principle between candidates (assortment, use situation, guided choice, expertise, story), but only principles that arise from the real domain. A sidebar instead of a top bar, cards instead of a list, or dark instead of light surfaces over the same information logic is the same candidate.

## 5. Design Navigation From Orientation Needs

Navigation answers: where am I, what belongs here, what can I do next, how do I reach a known target, how do I explore without one, and how do I return to an object, comparison, saved state or unfinished task. Combine global, local, contextual, sequential, search-led, spatial or object-centric roles where their boundaries are clear. Use user and domain language, keep mutually important destinations visible or quickly found, show location and parent and peer relationships where hierarchy matters, and keep labels on unfamiliar destinations. Choose a hamburger, mega menu, tab bar, command palette, rail or carousel for an orientation need, not because a template offers it, and match keyboard, screen-reader, touch, history and deep-link behavior to the information model.

## 6. Define Every Page by Its Job

Define each page's entry state, job, objects, decision readiness and place in the journey, then use [page-hierarchy.md](page-hierarchy.md) for its internal roles, order, copy and state-dependent grouping before components. No hero, sidebar or card count is mandatory.

## 7. Domain Architectures

- **Commerce** models the real buying decision rather than "hero + products + testimonials": assortment breadth, novice versus expert knowledge, selection criteria and compatibility, price, availability, fulfilment, returns, warranty and financing, desire and product truth, comparison and saved consideration, online, store, appointment or trial paths, and ownership after purchase. Imagery proves and creates desire while factual attributes stay legible; conversion may be purchase, reservation, test, configuration, contact or visit.
- **Service and transaction** journeys join eligibility, preparation, evidence, submission, progress, decision, recovery, support and status, without exposing the provider's structure. Explain relevance before demanding effort, show required evidence and time or cost early, keep entered work with a safe return path, place validation near the problem, state progress in plain language, and offer assisted routes when the digital path cannot finish. One question per page, long form, wizard, review screen and dashboard are candidates, not rules.
- **Editorial** architecture relates issues, articles, authors, topics, archives, collections, events, paid products and subscriber benefits, keeping reading momentum while discovery has clear roles: content voice carries identity, conversion waits for the reading task, archives are meaningful rather than generic card fields, and immersive motion appears only where it strengthens narrative and survives reduction.
- **Operational** architecture models objects, states, transitions, exceptions, urgency, ownership, history and repeated actions, optimizing detect → understand → act → confirm → recover. Keep decision context visible while acting and scan paths stable, use density where it cuts time and errors, support keyboard loops, bulk actions, undo and re-entry where evidence calls for them, and build dashboards from the relationships experts need rather than a default card grid.

## 8. Responsive Transformation

For every key experience, specify invariants (outcome, priority, reading and task order, required content, state, conversion); reorder and collapse rules; navigation transformation; disclosure changes and alternative controls; comparison and table behavior; image crop, substitution or removal; sticky behavior and safe areas; pointer, touch, keyboard, hover and gesture differences; and long content, localization, zoom and orientation. Place breakpoints where the composition or interaction stops working, and always test a pressure width between presets.

## 9. Architecture Proof

Test candidates with real content and the smallest slice that can fail them: first-time and returning entry; known-item and exploratory discovery; comparison with realistic differences; long labels and missing data; conversion readiness and hesitation; error, unavailable, out-of-stock or permission failure; mobile reordering and intermediate width; keyboard order and landmarks; cross-channel handoff and return. Reject an architecture that makes users understand the organization instead of their task, hides required evidence, splits one outcome across unrelated paths, depends on ideal content, or turns into a generic template once decoration is removed.

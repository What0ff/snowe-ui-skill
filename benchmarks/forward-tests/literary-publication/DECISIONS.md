# The Morrow Review — causal decisions

## Brief

An independent literary publication needs issue relationships, sustained reading, contributors, archive discovery, events, and membership. Editorial value and authorship must precede conversion.

## Chosen architecture

The current issue is the primary topology: opening thesis, lead story, complete contents, editorial context, then archive. Membership appears in the publication frame and after demonstrated editorial value, not as a product-grid interruption. Reading tools and translator notes live with the text they affect.

## Strongest rejected alternative

A catalog of article cards with a large image-led lead, category chips, trending rail, and subscription banner was rejected. It fragments the issue into interchangeable content units and erases sustained reading and translation relationships.

## Art direction and assets

Typography, issue numbering, editorial rules, marginalia, pace, and the authored text are the identity carrier. Generated imagery was rejected because invented illustration would compete with the fiction and imply a visual interpretation the publication did not commission. Custom assets and icons were also rejected; text labels and typographic structure are clearer and more ownable here.

## Motion

Only reading progress carries changing information. Menus, type size, save state, filtering, dialog, and validation update directly. There are no scroll reveals, carousels, ambient loops, or animated editorial decoration.

## Responsive transformation

The wide issue uses a vertical index, large opening thesis, marginal notes, and a narrow reading measure. At pressure width notes remain associated but move below their owner. On mobile the issue index becomes horizontal, the story and margin form one reading flow, contents retain sequence, and the archive becomes a ruled list rather than cards.

Opening story

Inventory of Small Thunder

In the summer the hydroelectric lake receded, and the village began returning one room at a time.

By Anca Dobre · translated by Sorin Vale

The first thing to surface was not the church tower, as everyone had predicted, but a blue enamel saucepan. It sat upright in the silt, holding three days of rain and the pale reflection of a power line.

My mother saw it from the new road. She asked me to stop the car, then stood at the guardrail with both hands on the hot metal as though she had arrived late to a bedside.

“Ours had a white rim,” she said.

Below us, the old valley had become a ledger. Stone foundations made columns through the mud. Orchard rows appeared as dark punctuation. Every afternoon another object entered the account: a door hinge, a shoe last, seven green pharmacy bottles, the iron ring where the ferry rope had been tied.

People came from the resettlement blocks carrying folding chairs. They watched the water release what it had held for forty-two years, and argued softly about ownership.

“Memory is a bad surveyor. It preserves the distance between two voices, then moves the house.”

On the fourth morning, my mother gave me a list. Not of objects she wanted recovered, she insisted, but of places we should stand while standing was possible.

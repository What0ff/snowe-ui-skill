# Larkhaven permit service — causal decisions

## Brief

A multilingual municipal service must help residents determine eligibility, prepare evidence, submit, recover, and understand status. Errors have real consequences; persuasion is not the job.

## Chosen architecture

The page is a service entry and eligibility checkpoint inside one visible end-to-end journey. The organizing object is the resident's request, not a city department or marketing narrative. Requirements precede the checker; the application CTA appears only after a result; status and assisted support remain adjacent recovery paths.

## Strongest rejected alternative

A city-homepage pattern with a large promotional hero, service cards, news, and a generic search box was rejected. It makes the resident rediscover the permit journey and hides recovery/status behind organizational navigation.

## Art direction and assets

Institutional clarity comes from civic typography, service reference bands, numbered process structure, and restrained paper/forest/amber materials. Generated imagery and custom graphics were rejected: neither can prove eligibility or reduce evidence errors better than content and visible service state. No icons are required for comprehension.

## Motion

No choreographed motion. Menus, validation, dialog, language, and results change state immediately with focus movement and live-region feedback. The reduced-motion branch disables any user-agent or future animation.

## Responsive transformation

Wide screens keep the request journey and assisted help visible beside the main service. At pressure width the aside becomes two panels after the task. On narrow screens facts, questions, results, and support form one linear evidence order; navigation becomes a labelled menu without changing service destinations.
